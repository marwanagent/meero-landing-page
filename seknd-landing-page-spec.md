# Seknd Landing Page: Build Specification

A build-ready spec for a single-page marketing site. This is the source of truth. Build against it.

Two setup tasks live outside the code and do not block the build: adding intake questions on the Calendly event, and choosing/pointing the domain. Both are noted in Section 10.

---

## 1. What this is and why it exists

A one-page site whose single job is to convert a visitor into a booked bottleneck-audit call via Calendly.

- **Audience:** owner-operators of businesses doing roughly $250K to $1M/year, where the owner is personally the bottleneck. They do the same recurring work every week because there is no one else to hand it to. They are not looking for software. They want the work off their plate.
- **The one action:** book a free bottleneck audit. No secondary CTAs compete with it.
- **What this is not:** not a feature tour, not a pricing page. It sells a conversation, and the conversation sells the build.

---

## 2. Positioning

Lead with what is different, not the payoff. Every competitor sells "buy back your time," so the emotional payoff (own your time, more time on the vision and with family) is the closing beat, not the opening promise. Open with the three differentiators:

1. **Custom.** Built around this specific business and the exact work this owner repeats. Not a template. Not software they configure.
2. **Done-for-you.** They do not build it, learn it, or maintain it. It is designed, deployed, and handed over working.
3. **Managed and monitored.** It runs on real infrastructure that is watched. When something breaks, it is caught and fixed. Not a fragile chain the owner babysits.

"Custom" carries the most weight and must be proven, not asserted: the examples are described as built for one operator's exact workflow, the audit is about the visitor's own week, and the proof section shows real rigor.

**Emphasis rule (important for tone):** the differentiators above all reduce the visitor's time and effort, which is the hard-to-copy part of the offer. Spend at least as much copy energy on "you do nothing and touch nothing" as on "imagine the freedom." That is where the offer is strong and where competitors are weak.

**Hard constraint:** the word "AI" appears nowhere in visible copy, alt text, meta tags, page title, or FAQ. See Section 8.

---

## 3. Persuasion structure

The page stacks six legitimate persuasion levers so they reinforce each other. Each is true, which is what lets them stack without feeling like a scam.

1. **Reciprocity** — the free audit is a real gift of value. Carried by the offer and every CTA.
2. **Authority through specificity** — not "reliable," but "runs on monitored infrastructure, retries automatically, checked every day." Carried by the proof section.
3. **Contrast** — the before/after "your week" visual makes value felt. Carried by Section (What changes for you).
4. **Social proof** — one true client quote. Carried by the proof section.
5. **Honest scarcity** — each build is custom and hands-on, so only a few clients at a time. True, therefore legitimate. Carried by the scarcity + CTA section.
6. **Commitment** — the audit is a small, easy first yes. Carried by the single low-friction CTA repeated at three scroll depths.

No fake urgency: no countdown timers, no "only N spots left," no exit-intent popups. The only scarcity is the honest statement in the scarcity section.

The audit is the lead magnet: a complete solution to a narrow problem ("I don't know what in my week could run without me"), given free, that reveals the larger need (once they see what could run without them, they want it built).

## 4. Page sections, in order, with final copy

Tone for all copy: warm, direct, human, plain verbs, sentence case, no filler, no hype, no jargon. Write from the owner's side of the screen: name things by what they control, never by how the system is built. No em dashes anywhere. The primary CTA repeats at three depths (hero, mid-page, bottom) so a visitor can act at any scroll position.

### The shift

Purpose: name the pain and separate the work they love from the work that just has to get done, so it never implies they dislike their business.

> You didn't start this business to spend your evenings on admin. You are good at the actual work, the part you started this to do, and you probably still love it. But the further you get, the more of your week disappears into the parts that just have to get done: the follow-ups, the invoicing, the inbox, the same information moved from one place to another again and again. And when you try to hand it off, you often just trade one time sink for another, because now you are hiring, training, and managing people to do work that should not need a person babysitting it. Either way, the week fills up and you are still the bottleneck. Every hour spent there is an hour not spent on the work that actually grows the business, or on the life you meant to have outside it.

### How it works

Numbered three-step sequence (numbering is appropriate here because it is a real sequence).

**1. We find your bottleneck.**
On a free call, we map the work you repeat every week and pinpoint what could run without you. You leave with that map whether or not we work together.

**2. We build it around your business.**
We design a custom system for your exact workflow, deploy it, and hand it over working. You do not build anything, learn anything, or move off the tools you already use.

**3. It runs, and you stay in control.**
It runs on its own on a schedule. You watch and control it from a simple interface on your phone or computer. When you want to change or improve it as you learn what works, we are right there.

## 5. Visual design direction: Option B, Ink & Paper

The feeling is calm reclaimed time and quiet competence: premium, trustworthy, human, deliberately not techy. Like the calm after you have handed something off, not a tech-startup dashboard.

**Avoid these (they read as generic or off-brief):**
- The three AI-design defaults: (a) cream background with high-contrast serif and terracotta accent, (b) near-black background with a single acid-green or vermilion accent, (c) broadsheet layout with hairline rules and dense columns.
- Generic SaaS tropes: gradient blobs, purple, floating 3D shapes, glowing nodes.
- Literal tech imagery: circuit boards, matrix rain, robots. We sell the calm, not the machine.

**Tokens (starting point, from the approved mockup):**
- Ink navy (primary text / structure / CTA fill): `#17203A`
- Cool paper (background): `#F4F5F7`
- Warm accent (CTA underline only, nowhere else): `#D99A4E`
- Muted body text: `#47506A`
- Card / stage background: `#FFFFFF`, hairline border `#DCE0E8`
- Display face: editorial serif, **Newsreader**, weight 500, tight leading. Body face: **Inter**.
- Restraint is the point: lots of whitespace, thin rules, warm accent only on the button.

**Signature element:** the animated pipeline (Graphic 1) is the one memorable, bold thing. Spend the boldness there; keep everything else quiet. If the page starts to feel busy, cut decoration before clarity.

**Hero execution (build right on the first pass, no second iteration):**
- **Type scale is the single biggest "editorial vs generic" lever.** Headline in Newsreader at a genuinely large editorial size: about `clamp(2.75rem, 5vw + 1rem, 4.5rem)`, weight 500, leading ~1.05, letter-spacing ~-0.01em, constrained to ~15ch so it breaks into two or three confident lines. Do not undersize it; a timid headline is what makes this direction read generic.
- **Left-align the hero, do not center it.** Centered blocks read as default SaaS.
- **Vertical rhythm:** generous top padding so the headline owns the first viewport. Eyebrow above the headline in Inter, small, uppercase, letter-spaced, muted navy. Subhead below in Inter ~18px, color `#47506A`, max ~48ch. CTA below that.
- **Pipeline placement:** a calm horizontal band directly below the hero copy, full content width, reading as a quiet demonstration under the thesis, not decoration beside it. On mobile it stacks below the CTA and connectors hide (stages wrap).
- **Color discipline:** warm accent only as the CTA underline. Everything else ink navy on cool paper with hairline dividers. Flat, precise, lots of whitespace. No gradients, no stacked shadows, no big-number-with-label block.
- **Generic vs right:** generic is a centered headline at a safe size, a gradient or stock illustration, a pill CTA, and three feature cards. Right is a large left-aligned serif headline that owns the screen, one muted subhead, one CTA with a warm underline, and the calm pipeline band beneath. Build the second one.

**Quality floor (non-negotiable):** responsive to 360px, visible keyboard focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, WCAG AA contrast, alt text on every visual.

## 6. Graphics

Three visuals, all built fresh and privacy-safe (they never expose real client data).

### Graphic 1 (hero): the animated pipeline

Concept: one task flows left to right through stages and completes, showing the shape of the work without any real content. This shows the machine working (proof of competence) and embodies "it runs on its own."

- Stages, left to right, as labeled nodes: `Find` -> `Research` -> `Personalize` -> `Send` -> `Follow up`. Plain-English labels, not technical terms.
- **Single token, not many.** Exactly one token travels the path left to right, once per cycle. No multiple tokens, no constant streaming. That is the "busy dashboard" failure to avoid.
- **Calm pacing is the brand.** One full traverse takes about 4 to 6 seconds, then a ~2-second rest holding the finished pipeline still, then repeat. Unhurried on purpose: the pace says "reliable, runs without you."
- **Easing:** ease-in-out throughout. A marble rolling along a track, never a progress bar racing. Nothing snappy, no bounce, no spin.
- **Stage completion:** as the token reaches each stage, its checkmark fades and scales in gently (~200 to 300ms) and the stage border firms up slightly. No color flash, no pop.
- **Connector:** the hairline between stages draws in left to right as the token passes. Keep it ink navy at low opacity (not the warm accent).
- **Restraint:** this is the ONLY animated thing on the page. Do not animate anything else, no scroll reveals competing with it.
- **Reduced motion:** when set, show the completed pipeline with all five checkmarks present, static, no token, no draw.
- Built as inline SVG + CSS keyframes (minimal JS only if needed to position the token, e.g. `offset-path` or translate across positioned nodes). No video, no heavy library. Must not delay first contentful paint.
- Accessible: aria-label describing the flow end to end.
- **Generic vs right:** generic is a glowing multi-node network with streaming particles and neon accents. Right is a quiet, near-monochrome ink-navy diagram where one token calmly completes a five-step journey and rests. Build the second one.

### Graphic 2: before/after "your week"

- **Left ("now"):** a week densely packed with recurring task blocks labeled in plain words ("Outreach," "Follow-ups," "Invoicing," "Inbox," "Admin"), visually crowded.
- **Right ("with Seknd"):** the same grid with those blocks removed or greyed as "handled," and the freed time relabeled with what the owner wants there ("Vision," "Growth," "Family," "Off").
- Contrast immediate and visceral: left heavy, right open.
- Mobile: stack vertically (now on top, after below) with a clear divider and label.
- Accessible: each grid has a text alternative.

### Graphic 3: illustrative sample outputs (appears twice)

Realistic mocks of the output the systems produce (an outreach message, a prospect brief, an invoice), written from scratch as examples.

- Each mock captioned "Illustrative example" so it never claims to be a real client's output. This is what makes it honest and privacy-safe.
- Light version in the examples section (one or two mocks). Fuller version in the proof section (a small gallery of three: personalized outreach message, one-page prospect brief, clean invoice).
- Content plausible and specific but entirely invented. No real names, no real client data.

## 7. Technical build

**Stack:**
- Framework: **Next.js, static.** Chosen over Astro because Marwan will not touch the code, so generation and deploy reliability outweigh Astro's marginally lighter payload (negligible on one well-built page). Next is Vercel-native and the most reliably generated framework.
- Styling: Tailwind CSS.
- Hosting: **Vercel.** Do not host on the Hetzner agent box; a marketing page needs edge speed and should not share infrastructure with production agent pipelines.
- Booking: **Calendly inline embed** (preferred over popup so the action is always visible).
  - URL (store in the single content file): `https://calendly.com/marwan-treehousemusic/bottleneck-analysis-call`
  - The event carries 2 to 3 custom intake questions so the prospect's answers arrive with the booking (this is a Calendly-side config, see Section 10). A standalone contact form is rejected: it adds the exact delay and effort the page exists to remove.
- Analytics: Plausible or Umami (privacy-friendly, no cookie banner needed). Fire events on CTA click and on booking completion.

**Single source of truth for content:** all copy, the Calendly URL, and all design tokens live in exactly one place each (a content file, e.g. `src/content/site.ts`, and the Tailwind config + tokens file). No copy string and no hex value duplicated across components.

**Structure:** single route (`/`), componentized by section (Hero, Shift, HowItWorks, Examples, BeforeAfter, Proof, Control, Scarcity, FAQ, Footer). The CTA is one shared component reused at all three depths, pointing at the single Calendly URL from the content file.

## 8. Constraints (hard rules)

- The word "AI" appears nowhere in visible copy, alt text, meta description, page title, or FAQ.
- No fabricated metrics and no invented testimonials beyond what is in this spec.
- No fake urgency: no countdown timers, no "only N spots left," no exit-intent popups.
- The social example is described by outcome only; its mechanism is never described.
- Every claim on the page must be one Marwan can defend on a sales call.
- No em dashes anywhere.
- Describe everything from the owner's side of the screen.

---

## 9. Failure modes to handle

- **Calendly embed fails to load.** Show a visible fallback link to the booking URL, never a dead button or blank space. The CTA must always lead somewhere.
- **Analytics blocked or fails.** Page works fully regardless; analytics is never in the critical path.
- **Slow first paint.** Target Lighthouse performance >= 90. The hero animation must not block first contentful paint.
- **Mobile layout breakage.** Must render correctly at 360px. Test the before/after grid and the pipeline animation specifically.
- **Reduced motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` produces a clean static hero, not a broken or empty frame.
- **JS disabled.** Core content and the CTA still render and function (the Calendly link works as a plain link).
- **CTA lost on scroll.** The primary CTA appears at three depths; verify each is reachable and functional.
- **Placeholder text shipped.** All copy in this spec is final. Keep a pre-launch grep for `[[` and fail the build on any match, as a safety net.

## 10. Setup tasks outside the code (do not block the build)

- **Calendly intake questions.** Add 2 to 3 questions on the bottleneck-audit event. Suggested: (1) What's your business and what does it do? (2) What recurring work eats the most of your week? (3) Roughly what's your annual revenue (optional, helps pre-qualify).
- **Domain.** Choose and point the domain, then set it on the Vercel project.

## 11. Definition of done

1. Lighthouse >= 90 on performance, accessibility, best-practices, and SEO.
2. A real test booking lands in the connected calendar.
3. CTA-click and booking-completion fire as analytics events.
4. Renders correctly on iOS Safari and Android Chrome at 360px, with the before/after grid and pipeline animation verified specifically.
5. `prefers-reduced-motion` produces a clean static hero.
6. No console errors.
7. All copy, the Calendly URL, and design tokens live in a single place each; no duplication.
8. No `[[` placeholder text remains in the source (verified by the pre-launch grep).
9. The word "AI" does not appear anywhere in the rendered page or its metadata.
10. Every CTA leads to a working booking action, including the fallback if the embed fails.
